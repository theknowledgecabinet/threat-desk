Slovenian meat plant freezer hacked, set to 100°C

Attackers altered cooling settings at Salamerija Alpija and blocked technician access, highlighting risks in internet-connected operational technology.

Hackers changed the temperature setting for a freezer chamber at Salamerija Alpija, a meat processing plant in Tolmin, Slovenia, to 100°C. When a service technician attempted to sign in to the cooling control system, access was blocked, and the screen displayed a photo of Italian minister Matteo Salvini with the caption “Thank Uncle Matteo,” according to cooperative director Nikola Šavle.

Staff detected the change during a routine check before the actual chamber temperature rose, preventing spoilage. The cooperative estimated that approximately €250,000 worth of goods were at risk. Further checks revealed that a refrigerator at one of their stores had also been set to 20°C instead of the usual 2–3°C. The cooperative disconnected the cooling equipment from the network and switched to manual temperature control.

The incident shares features with recent attacks on European supermarket refrigeration systems, though no public evidence links this specific event to the Danfoss commercial refrigeration controller advisory published by CISA on 24 November. The cooperative plans to report the incident to Slovenia’s national cyber response centre, SI-CERT.

Affected

Internet-connected cooling control systems at Salamerija Alpija

Actions

  • Inventory operational technology assets to identify which controllers serve each room and how external teams connect.
  • Remove direct access from the public internet and separate control equipment from business networks.
  • Implement independent sensors to alert staff when room temperatures leave approved ranges, regardless of controller settings.
  • Practise manual operation to ensure staff can maintain safe equipment operation if remote access is lost.
