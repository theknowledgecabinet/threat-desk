UK Cyber Security and Resilience Bill faces Commons scrutiny

The CSRB, which expands regulatory scope to include managed service providers and datacentres, is being debated in the House of Commons amid criticism that it leaves major commercial sectors and AI risks unaddressed.

The Cyber Security and Resilience Bill (CSRB) is being considered in the House of Commons on 24 November 2026, following its third reading in the House of Lords on 2 November. The legislation represents the most significant update to the Network and Information Systems (NIS) regulations since 2018, bringing managed service providers (MSPs), datacentres, and large load-controllers into regulatory scope alongside essential operators in energy, water, transport, and health.

Key provisions include compressing incident reporting to an initial alert within 24 hours and a fuller report within 72 hours, granting ministers powers to issue directions, and raising maximum penalties to £17m or 4% of global turnover. The bill also mandates a 12-month review of the NIS regime and establishes a register of state actors posing cyber threats to the UK.

Lord Chris Holmes, a member of the House of Lords, argues the bill is "significantly insufficient" in critical areas, noting that major commercial enterprises such as retail and the motor industry remain outside the regulatory perimeter. He highlights the absence of provisions for board accountability, skills development, and protections for good-faith cyber professionals, as well as the lack of a single, horizontally focused regulator for emerging technologies like AI.

Affected

Network and Information Systems (NIS) regulations; Cyber Security and Resilience Bill

Actions

  • Review incident reporting procedures to ensure compliance with the new 24-hour initial alert and 72-hour full report requirements.
  • Assess whether your organisation falls within the expanded scope of essential operators, MSPs, datacentres, or designated critical suppliers.
  • Monitor the House of Commons debate for amendments that may affect board accountability and remediation responsibilities.
