Shadowserver finds 4,200 exposed AI hacking agents

The Shadowserver Foundation identified 4,200 internet-exposed instances of AI attack frameworks, with ARTEX accounting for roughly 70% of the observed deployments.

CVE-2023-38646

Shadowserver’s 25 November 2026 report documented 4,200 internet-exposed instances of AI hacking frameworks, an increase from just under 3,700 the previous day. The organisation recorded more than 10,000 events from over 80 IP addresses between 9 and 23 November, noting that these figures measure attempted activity against honeypots rather than confirmed breaches. ARTEX and its derivative AntiFox comprised about three-quarters of observed instances on 22 November, while a jump in PentestCode deployments drove the subsequent increase.

Nearly nine in ten attacking IP addresses were fingerprinted as ARTEX Gateway. Activity was concentrated in the United States and Hong Kong, likely reflecting server hosting locations rather than operator bases. About six in ten source addresses attempted to relay traffic, while direct requests probed VPN gateways, web applications, and developer tools such as Jenkins. Approximately one in ten source addresses sent exploit or injection attempts, most frequently targeting the Metabase vulnerability CVE-2023-38646.

The FBI’s 13 November advisory stated that some ARTEX-assisted intrusions moved from initial targeting to privileged access in under three hours. Shadowserver advises security teams to check its Device Identification report for the 'ai-attack-framework' label. If a deployment is authorised, access should be limited to trusted addresses or a VPN with strong authentication; if unverified, the system should be isolated and investigated for misuse.

Affected

Metabase open source before 0.46.6.1 and Metabase Enterprise before 1.46.6.1

Actions

  • Check Shadowserver’s Device Identification report for the 'ai-attack-framework' label to identify exposed instances.
  • Limit access to authorised AI attack frameworks to trusted addresses or a VPN and enforce strong authentication.
  • Isolate and investigate any AI framework deployments that cannot be verified as authorised.
  • Rate-limit repeated probes and ensure alerts are handled outside business hours.
