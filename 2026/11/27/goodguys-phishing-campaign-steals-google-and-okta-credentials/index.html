GoodGuys phishing campaign steals Google and Okta credentials

Push Security identified a campaign using fake paid expert interviews to harvest corporate SSO credentials via custom phishing kits.

Push Security has tracked a phishing campaign dubbed "GoodGuys" since 13 October 2026. The threat actors impersonate eight organisations, including Google Cloud, Nvidia, Accenture, and SpaceX, to distribute fake invitations for paid expert interviews or webinars. Recipients are offered up to $600 for a 45-minute session, luring them into signing in with their work accounts to validate their participation.

The campaign abuses legitimate email and event platforms such as HireVue, SendGrid, Mandrill, and Cvent to deliver phishing links. These links direct victims to brand-specific subdomains hosting customised phishing pages. Unlike adversary-in-the-middle kits that use reverse proxies, GoodGuys employs fake authentication forms controlled by an attacker server. The kit specifically targets corporate accounts, rejecting personal email domains like Gmail, Yahoo, and Outlook.

After victims enter their credentials, the phishing page displays multifactor authentication challenges, including SMS codes, authenticator codes, and Google sign-in prompts. Push Security believes the threat actors manually attempt to log in using the stolen credentials, instructing the page to relay whatever MFA or security challenges Google or Okta present. The researchers noted that hidden accessibility labels in the kit’s code suggest it may have been developed using an AI coding assistant.

Affected

Corporate Google and Okta accounts

Actions

  • Verify the authenticity of interview or webinar invitations through official channels before clicking links.
  • Monitor for unexpected MFA prompts or login attempts from unfamiliar locations.
  • Educate staff that legitimate organisations rarely offer cash payments for short expert interviews via unsolicited emails.
