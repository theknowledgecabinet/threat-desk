Suspected ransomware disrupts Guadeloupe hospital, forcing manual care

A suspected ransomware attack has impaired systems at the University Hospital of Guadeloupe, suspending cancer treatments and surgeries while ANSSI investigates a potential supply chain compromise.

France’s national cybersecurity agency, ANSSI, is investigating a suspected ransomware attack that impaired the computer systems of the University Hospital of Guadeloupe. The incident, which began with computer and telephone outages on Saturday evening, turned out to be a cyberattack that encrypted the hospital’s databases. ANSSI is examining whether attackers gained access through an unnamed IT supplier that was reportedly compromised several months ago. French prosecutors have launched a criminal investigation, though the perpetrators remain unidentified.

The disruption has forced medical staff to revert to manual processes, with the hospital director stating, “We’re back to pen and paper, relying on couriers who travel from one department to another.” As of Tuesday, healthcare workers could not access electronic medical records, leading to the cancellation of some surgical procedures, medical consultations, and imaging tests through at least Friday. Radiation therapy was temporarily suspended for safety reasons, and chemotherapy sessions scheduled for Monday were cancelled. Emergency services, dialysis, and urgent cardiac procedures remain available, but the hospital has asked residents to visit emergency departments only for immediate care.

The hospital management stated it had not received a ransom demand and ruled out paying one. It remains unclear whether personal or medical information was stolen, though the hospital warned patients to watch for phishing attempts by criminals posing as staff. Recovery is expected to take several days as the hospital checks systems for malware before gradually restoring operations. The attack occurred while the hospital was relocating to a new facility in Belle-Plaine, which management said has stronger security protections.

Actions

  • Monitor for phishing attempts from actors posing as hospital staff.
  • Bring paper copies of medical records, prescriptions, and test results to appointments.
  • Limit non-urgent visits to emergency departments during the recovery period.
