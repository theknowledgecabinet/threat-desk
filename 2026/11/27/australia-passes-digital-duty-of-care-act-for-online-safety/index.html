Australia passes Digital Duty of Care Act for online safety

Australia has enacted legislation requiring online providers to implement protocols preventing foreseeable harms, including threats of violence and rape.

The Digital Duty of Care Act has passed through Australia's Parliament, imposing new obligations on major technology companies. The legislation mandates that online providers establish specific protocols to protect users from threats of violence and rape, as well as behaviour that could cause significant harm.

According to The Record, the law is designed to preempt foreseeable harms rather than simply reacting to incidents after they occur. It targets large online platforms, requiring them to take proactive steps in their safety frameworks.

Actions

  • Review compliance requirements for Australian operations under the new Digital Duty of Care Act.
  • Audit existing safety protocols to ensure they address threats of violence and significant harm.
