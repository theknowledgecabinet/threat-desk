California Privacy Protection Agency director Tom Kemp to depart

Tom Kemp is leaving the CalPrivacy directorship after two years, having overseen the launch of the DROP platform and significant privacy legislation.

Tom Kemp, director of the California Privacy Protection Agency (CalPrivacy), announced his departure on Tuesday after nearly two years in the role. He will begin a new position in January, though he did not disclose details of the role in his LinkedIn post.

During his tenure, Kemp highlighted the implementation of the Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform (DROP), which debuted in January. The system is now used by more than 600,000 Californians to block data sharing and force deletions by data brokers, with each user averaging more than 200 deletions. Penalties for companies violating these privacy wishes took effect in August.

Kemp stated that CalPrivacy made significant progress in making it easier for Californians to exercise privacy rights. Under his leadership, the agency also pushed for two pieces of legislation expanding consumer privacy, lobbied against federal preemption of California’s laws, and oversaw the issuance of the largest fine in the agency’s history. Additionally, CalPrivacy adopted regulations mandating how industry deploys cybersecurity audits, risk assessments, and automated decision-making technology.

Actions

  • Monitor state-level privacy regulations for changes in enforcement timelines.
  • Ensure data processing practices align with state-specific opt-out mechanisms like DROP.
  • Review automated decision-making and AI deployment against new regulatory standards.
