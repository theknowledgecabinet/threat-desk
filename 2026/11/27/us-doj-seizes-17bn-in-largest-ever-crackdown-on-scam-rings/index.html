US DOJ seizes $17bn in largest-ever crackdown on scam rings

The US Department of Justice announced it has targeted over 800 alleged scammers and seized nearly $17 billion in illicit funds, marking the largest enforcement action against fraud targeting Americans.

The Department of Justice stated that the operation covers more than 400 cases across 85 federal districts, resulting in new charges, guilty verdicts, and the extradition of 30 defendants. The initiative, launched following Executive Order 14390 in March 2026, involved US prosecutors and agents working with police in more than 10 countries to raid scam call centres and compounds abroad.

Under Operation Blackout, the FBI reported seizing nearly $17 billion in illicit funds, dismantling more than 29 scam compounds, and assisting in the repatriation of over 2,200 trafficked workers. The bureau also warned more than 11,000 Americans that they were being targeted and estimated it prevented over $690 million in losses. The State Department helped freeze over $1 billion in stolen cryptocurrency and take down more than 1.4 million fraudulent websites and accounts, while the Federal Trade Commission returned almost $3 billion to consumers.

Specific operations included the prosecution of gang members in Georgia prisons who allegedly used smuggled phones to demand fines from victims claiming missed jury duty. The Federal Communications Commission approved a rule on 19 November allowing prisons to use jamming technology against contraband phones. Other cases involved the sale of personal data of elderly Americans and a Peruvian call centre targeting Spanish-speaking victims with threats of deportation.

Actions

  • Monitor for unsolicited calls demanding payment for missed jury duty or deportation threats.
  • Verify the identity of callers claiming to be from government agencies before sharing personal details.
  • Report suspected scam activity to the Federal Trade Commission or local law enforcement.
