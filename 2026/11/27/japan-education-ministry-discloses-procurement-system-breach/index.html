Japan education ministry discloses procurement system breach

MEXT confirmed an intrusion into its government procurement database, potentially exposing business records and email addresses of companies eligible for tenders.

Japan’s Ministry of Education, Culture, Sports, Science and Technology (MEXT) disclosed that hackers breached its government procurement system, which is used to publish information about tenders, contracts and procurement opportunities. The ministry stated it detected the intrusion on 1 October, nearly two months before the public announcement.

According to MEXT, the potentially compromised data includes nearly 1,700 email addresses and 164,000 records containing company names, employee names and other business information relating to companies eligible to participate in the ministry’s bidding processes. The ministry said it had blocked all communications between the affected system and internal and external networks.

MEXT reported no evidence that the potentially compromised information has been publicly disclosed or misused. The incident was reported to Japan’s data protection authorities, and the ministry is continuing its investigation in coordination with relevant government agencies. It has not publicly identified the perpetrators or disclosed how they gained access to its systems.

Affected

MEXT government procurement system

Actions

  • Monitor for unusual activity in procurement and tender databases.
  • Verify the integrity of data stored in government-facing public systems.
  • Ensure network segmentation between public-facing systems and internal networks.
