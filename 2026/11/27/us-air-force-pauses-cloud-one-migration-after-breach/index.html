US Air Force pauses Cloud One migration after breach

The US Air Force has blocked updates and paused migrations on its Cloud One platform after discovering an intrusion that may expose sensitive military data.

Reuters reports that hackers breached Cloud One, the US Air Force’s flagship cloud computing platform. The intrusion was discovered approximately one month ago during upgrade work. The Air Force has blocked updates to applications running on the platform, leaving personnel unable to install fixes for newly disclosed software vulnerabilities.

The scope of the breach remains unknown. Reuters could not identify the attackers, determine how much of the platform was affected, or confirm whether data was stolen. One source said the attackers exploited a software flaw to access the platform’s control plane, the layer used to manage main functions. Investigators have asked US Cyber Command to assess the damage.

The incident has disrupted operations, including the pause of migrations for back-office applications to Cloud One. Some users reportedly had their administrator rights revoked. The Air Force stated it does not comment on the security posture or operational status of specific networks and systems.

Affected

US Air Force Cloud One

Actions

  • Review cloud audit logs to distinguish account access from actual activity.
  • Verify the status of application updates and patching capabilities.
  • Assess the impact of blocked updates on known vulnerabilities.
