Florida data recovery co-founder charged with wire fraud

Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar is accused of secretly paying ransomware gangs while telling victims his firm had decryption technology, profiting from the difference.

The US Department of Justice has indicted Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, a 52-year-old co-founder of a Florida data recovery firm, on three counts of wire fraud. Prosecutors allege that Escobar told clients his company possessed "sophisticated decryption technology" to recover data without payment, but instead negotiated with attackers behind their backs. He is accused of paying the ransomware groups and then charging his clients a higher amount, using the proceeds to purchase properties in Florida.

The case relies on an undercover operation in June 2026, where FBI agents posed as a ransomware victim and the attacker. According to the indictment, Escobar allegedly paid the fake attacker approximately $6,000 in Bitcoin for a decryption key, then charged the undercover company $13,260. First Assistant US Attorney Charles C. Calenda stated that the defendant "preyed on ransomware victims by falsely marketing a solution he did not possess."

Escobar was arrested in Las Vegas on 22 October and faces a maximum of 20 years in prison per count. This follows the July sentencing of Angelo Martino, a former DigitalMint negotiator, who was sentenced to 70 months for passing confidential victim details to BlackCat (ALPHV) operators.

Actions

  • Verify the credentials and technical capabilities of third-party data recovery firms before engaging.
  • Ensure contractual agreements explicitly prohibit third parties from negotiating with or paying ransomware actors.
  • Monitor financial transactions for discrepancies between what is paid to attackers and what is billed to the client.
