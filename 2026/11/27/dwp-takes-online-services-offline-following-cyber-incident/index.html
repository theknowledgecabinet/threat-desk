DWP takes online services offline following cyber incident

The UK Department for Work and Pensions has disabled key digital services as a precaution after detecting a cyber incident, with no evidence of data loss reported so far.

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) confirmed on 27 November 2026 that it had taken several online services offline as a precaution following a cyber incident. The department, which administers state pensions, universal credit, and disability benefits to approximately 20 million claimants, stated that regular benefit and pension payments are unaffected and will continue as normal. Services for claiming pensions, universal credit, and personal independence payments were impacted, with users advised to try again later or use telephone contacts.

Downing Street said an investigation was underway to establish the cause of the incident, noting that it could not rule out a malicious attack. The prime minister’s spokesperson described the situation as a “live and ongoing incident” and stated there was “currently no evidence of data loss.” The DWP is working with the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) and the Government Cyber Coordination Centre (GC3) to investigate and restore services. The Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) is engaging with the DWP to establish the circumstances, though no breach has been reported to date.

The incident follows recent warnings from the UK government regarding the threat landscape, including NCSC observations of a marked rise in AI-assisted cyberattacks since September. Chi Onwurah, chair of the Science and Technology Committee, described the disruption as a wake-up call, urging the government to address weaknesses in digital infrastructure and improve data hygiene standards.

Affected

DWP online services (Universal Credit, State Pensions, Personal Independence Payments)

Actions

  • Monitor DWP official channels for service restoration updates.
  • Use telephone contact numbers provided by DWP for urgent claims or queries.
  • Do not click links or provide bank details in response to unsolicited messages claiming to be from DWP.
  • Review incident response plans for dependencies on government digital services.
