Bithumb confirms 15-day breach of internal collaboration system

South Korean exchange Bithumb disclosed that attackers accessed its DaouOffice system for 15 days, stealing passport numbers, bank details, and wallet addresses, raising significant phishing risks.

Bithumb said in a 25 November update that its investigation revealed unauthorised access to its DaouOffice collaboration system from 12 to 27 October, extending far beyond the initially reported one-hour window. The company stated that the breach exposed personal information and files, including copies of identity documents, South Korean resident registration numbers, driver’s license numbers, transaction histories, and contact details.

The affected individuals include customers, employees of partner companies, and current and former staff. Bithumb said the stolen data varied by person and that the initial disclosure covered about 7,100 customers and employees, though it did not provide a new total in the updated notice. The exchange noted that DaouOffice is separate from its trading systems and that it found no evidence of a direct breach of the exchange or its customer database.

Bithumb said it blocked the IP addresses tied to the intrusion, suspended DaouOffice, and worked with the software developer to investigate. The company stated it fixed the security flaw and is reviewing its internet-facing services. It is notifying affected customers by email and text and will tell each person which records were stolen.

Affected

Bithumb DaouOffice collaboration system

Actions

  • Verify any security alerts through the official app or customer centre rather than following links in messages.
  • Do not share passwords or one-time codes, install apps at a caller’s request, or move crypto to a so-called safe wallet.
  • Monitor bank accounts and replace identity documents if copies were stolen, as per Bithumb’s guidance.
