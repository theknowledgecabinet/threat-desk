Ransomware hits Port of Santos and Argentinian pipeline

Industrial sectors in Latin America face a surge in ransomware, with recent attacks affecting major infrastructure in Brazil and Argentina.

On 6 November, a ransomware attack encrypted data at the Port of Santos in Brazil, the largest port in South America. A simultaneous power outage at the port's data centre is under investigation, though the Santos Port Authority (APS) stated that operations were not disrupted, mainly because the authority switched to alternate processes. APS restored its enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems and customer services on 24 November, noting that the intruder likely gained entry via a malicious email or infected website.

The incident is part of a broader trend in the region. Dragos reported that industrial organisations in South America suffered at least 82 claimed ransomware incidents in the third quarter of 2026, up from 64 in the previous quarter. Brazil recorded 22 incidents, while Peru and Argentina each tallied 16. Abdul Alamri, a principal threat intelligence analyst at Dragos, noted that while operational technology (OT) systems were not directly compromised in these cases, disruptions to enterprise IT and ERP systems often cascade into operational stoppages.

In Argentina, pipeline company Oldelval reported a cybersecurity incident affecting administrative systems, according to filings with the National Securities Commission. The company stated that the movement of oil and crude was not hampered. However, Alamri cautioned that statements claiming only administrative systems were affected should be read with caution, as incidents classified as IT-only can still impact engineering workstations and human-machine interface hardware running Windows.

Affected

Port of Santos ERP systems; Oldelval administrative systems

Actions

  • Monitor enterprise IT and ERP systems for ransomware indicators, as these are primary targets for disrupting industrial operations.
  • Verify that backups are intact and recoverable, noting that data loss may occur for periods prior to the last successful backup.
  • Review access logs for potential entry via malicious email or compromised web portals.
  • Assess the impact of IT disruptions on operational technology, including engineering workstations and HMIs, even if core OT systems appear unaffected.
