NCC Group: Ransomware claims rise 23% in October

A new report indicates a sharp increase in claimed ransomware incidents, driven by AI-assisted vulnerability discovery and the emergence of new extortion brands.

NCC Group has reported that claimed ransomware attacks surged by 23% in October 2026, reaching 1,049 incidents. This follows a record-breaking third quarter for 2026. The researchers attribute the spike to the growing use of artificial intelligence to rapidly identify vulnerabilities and chained attack paths, describing it as an early indicator of a new cyber environment where serious flaws are weaponised more quickly.

The Gentlemen ransomware group claimed the highest number of victims in October with 139 incidents, followed closely by N0n, a new actor first observed in September 2026, which claimed 133 victims. N0n has also claimed responsibility for data breaches affecting JPMorgan, Mastercard, and Visa. Qilin placed third with 131 claimed attacks. NCC Group noted that several newer groups established in 2026, including Booba Project, Emperador, and Wallstreet, made up the rest of the top 10, though their origins remain unknown and they have not been conclusively linked to specific countries or predecessors.

The industrials sector was the most heavily targeted, accounting for 31% of claimed attacks, followed by consumer discretionary (19%), healthcare (12%), and IT (10%). North America was the most targeted region, representing 46% of attacks, with Europe following at 23%.

Actions

  • Monitor threat intelligence for new ransomware brands and their claimed victim lists.
  • Prioritise remediation of critical vulnerabilities in industrial and IT systems.
  • Review third-party risk management, particularly for financial and industrial supply chains.
