Switzerland confirms first AI-agent cyberattacks on critical infrastructure

Swiss and Italian agencies report successful intrusions using autonomous AI agents, urging immediate patching of internet-facing systems.

Switzerland’s National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) has confirmed its first reports of cyberattacks against local organisations carried out with the assistance of AI agents. The agency stated that in every known case, the use of these agents led to the successful compromise of the victim’s IT systems, affecting operators in at least two critical infrastructure sectors. The NCSC noted that the number of publicly available AI agents accessible over the internet has nearly doubled since October 2026.

The attacks utilised what the NCSC terms “Agentic Offensive Frameworks,” which can search for vulnerabilities, select attack tools, and adapt tactics with limited human input. CSIRT Italia reported similar large-scale campaigns where multiple AI agents worked in parallel to compromise systems within hours, chaining low-impact vulnerabilities into complex attack chains. The agencies identified ARTEX, CyberStrikeAI, DeepSeek Harness - Red Team, and PentAGI as frameworks involved in recent incidents.

The NCSC warns that these tools significantly reduce the time available for defenders to detect and respond to intrusions. It advises organisations to reduce their internet attack surface, restrict access via geo-blocking or IP allow lists, and ensure all internal resources reachable from the internet, such as webmail and VPNs, are protected by multi-factor authentication or passkeys.

Affected

Internet-facing systems, critical infrastructure operators, ARTEX, CyberStrikeAI, DeepSeek Harness - Red Team, PentAGI

Actions

  • Install critical security updates for internet-accessible systems within 24 hours of release.
  • Reduce the number of systems exposed to the internet and restrict access using geo-blocking or IP allow lists.
  • Enforce multi-factor authentication or passkeys for all internal services reachable from the internet.
  • Monitor for deviations from normal behaviour and data volumes across identities and infrastructure.
