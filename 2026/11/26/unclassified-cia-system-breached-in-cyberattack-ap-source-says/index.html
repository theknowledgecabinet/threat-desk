Unclassified CIA system breached in cyberattack, AP source says

A person familiar with the incident told the Associated Press that an unclassified CIA system handling open-source intelligence was compromised, though no classified data was accessed.

An unclassified system at the US Central Intelligence Agency was breached in a cyberattack, according to a person familiar with the incident who spoke to The Associated Press on condition of anonymity. The source stated that the breach did not involve any classified information and that the issue has been resolved.

The affected system handles open-source intelligence, which involves the collection and analysis of publicly available data from sources including the internet, commercial satellites and private data brokers. While this information is generally public or semi-public, it can provide intelligence analysts with significant insight when combined with data from classified programmes.

It remains unclear what specific information was compromised or who was responsible for the attack. The incident was first reported by The New York Times on Tuesday and comes amid a series of other high-profile system failures and outages affecting US critical infrastructure and major technology firms.

Actions

  • Monitor for unusual activity in systems handling open-source intelligence or public data aggregation.
  • Verify that incident response procedures are tested and ready for rapid containment of potential breaches.
  • Review access controls and logging for systems that process semi-public data to detect unauthorised access.
