Aikido finds 42 phishing pages on Hugging Face

Attackers used Hugging Face's DeepSite AI to create fake login pages for 28 brands, including Microsoft and Google, to harvest credentials.

Aikido Security identified 42 pages on Hugging Face Spaces that impersonated 28 brands, including Microsoft, Google, PayPal, Chase, and Coinbase. Researchers linked 29 of these pages to DeepSite, an AI website builder, through project tags and change records. The pages were hosted at hf.space addresses, using HTTPS encryption that did not verify brand ownership.

Aikido discovered the pages on 16 October 2026 and reported them to Hugging Face the same day. At that time, 34 pages were live and eight were paused. By 29 October 2026, all 42 pages were paused or no longer publicly reachable. Researchers noted that one fake Microsoft login page had 15 recorded changes, 14 of which occurred within approximately 90 minutes, suggesting automated generation rather than manual coding.

Some pages requested one-time sign-in codes, card details, or personal information in addition to passwords. One Microsoft clone redirected visitors to the real Outlook site after collecting their information. Researchers could not determine how many users interacted with the pages or entered credentials, as visitor records were unavailable.

Affected

Hugging Face Spaces

Actions

  • Search DNS, proxy, firewall, and email gateway logs for reported phishing hostnames.
  • Reset passwords and revoke active sessions if users may have submitted credentials.
  • Flag unexpected hf.space links for review.
  • Instruct users to access services via saved bookmarks or organisation app portals rather than email links.
