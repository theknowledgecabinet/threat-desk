CISA warns of critical unpatched flaw in Danfoss refrigeration controllers

CISA has issued an advisory for a critical, unpatched buffer overflow in Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA controllers, which have recently been targeted in attacks across European supermarkets.

CVE-2026-129713

The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) issued an ICS advisory on 24 November regarding CVE-2026-129713, a stack-based buffer overflow in the Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA Series controllers. The vulnerability carries a CVSS score of 9.8 and allows remote attackers to send specially crafted HTTP/S POST requests without authentication, leading to memory corruption, web server restart, and potential arbitrary code execution with web server privileges.

Danfoss confirmed on 20 November that the flaw will be addressed in upcoming software version 5.5.x, but no patch is currently available. The update will also remove the option for customers to disable 'Strict mode'. CISA notes that the controllers are deployed worldwide, though the exact number remains unclear.

This advisory follows two waves of attacks in recent weeks linked to Iranian state actors. Between 11 and 12 October, French supermarkets suffered losses after attacks attributed to Iran’s IRGC-linked Shahid Kaveh unit compromised Danfoss and Copeland/Dixell controllers to alter defrost settings. A second wave on 1 and 2 November affected at least 115 stores across Belgium, the Netherlands, Sweden, Luxembourg, Ireland, Romania, and Estonia, with supplier Sabcobel stating the attackers used the same vulnerability seen in the earlier French incidents.

Affected

Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA Series (820A, 850A, 880A models)

Actions

  • Keep controllers off the public internet and minimise network exposure.
  • Segregate control systems from business networks and place them behind firewalls.
  • Use up-to-date Virtual Private Networks (VPNs) when remote access is required.
  • Configure all affected devices to operate in Strict mode.
