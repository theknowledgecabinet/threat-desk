ShinyHunters claims return via new Tor site after member arrests

The extortion group has relaunched its data leak site, using historical PGP keys to assert legitimacy following a series of international arrests targeting its members.

ShinyHunters has announced its return on a new Tor-based data leak site launched on 23 November 2026, following a period of inactivity after law enforcement operations targeted alleged members. The group’s statement, published in Russian and English, distances itself from former members and denounces those who have been arrested and are reportedly cooperating with authorities. The operators claim to be the original group, stating that "a lot has happened recently" and noting that their main representative had stopped responding and their Telegram account was deleted.

To verify their identity, the new operators signed verification messages using two historical ShinyHunters PGP keys dating back to 2020. BleepingComputer confirmed that the signatures match known public keys associated with the group, including one used to authenticate communications in August 2025. However, these keys were revoked in March 2026, and the group stated at that time that the older keys would no longer be used. The new announcement also introduces a new PGP key, but notably, none of the messages were signed with the March 2026 replacement key, leaving it unclear if the new operators possess that specific private key.

The group’s return follows the arrest of several individuals linked to ShinyHunters, including a 24-year-old Amsterdam man identified in media reports as Pepijn van der Stap (Umbreon), a member known as Rey or Saif reportedly detained in Jordan, and Canadian cybersecurity executive Edward Dubrovsky arrested in Pennsylvania. The new statement mocks these individuals, suggesting their arrests resulted from "silly mistakes" and involvement with "the wrong people." The operators say they will now communicate exclusively through a specific account on the Exploit hacking forum and the new Tor site, and plan to launch a new CDN for hosting stolen data.

Actions

  • Monitor threat intelligence feeds for new ShinyHunters PGP keys and data leak site URLs.
  • Verify the authenticity of any communications claiming to be from ShinyHunters by checking PGP signatures against known, unrevoked keys.
  • Review access logs for potential compromise of systems that may have been targeted by the group prior to the arrests.
