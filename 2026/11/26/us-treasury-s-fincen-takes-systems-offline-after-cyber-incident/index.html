US Treasury's FinCEN takes systems offline after cyber incident

The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network has disconnected some systems to investigate unauthorised access, causing temporary service disruptions and filing deadline extensions.

The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), the US Treasury Department’s financial intelligence unit, has disconnected some of its systems while investigating a cyber incident. In a notice on the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) E-Filing System website, the agency stated it is investigating a cybersecurity incident that resulted in unauthorised access to a limited subset of its systems.

As a proactive measure, FinCEN has taken some systems offline. The website remains operational, but some FinCEN services may be temporarily unavailable. The agency has extended deadlines for qualifying filers affected by the disruption, granting temporary, limited exception relief in response to the service hindering reporting obligations.

FinCEN has not shared further details on the nature of the incident or made any mention of a potential data breach. It is currently unclear who was behind the attack or if any data was accessed or stolen. The agency said it is working to return all systems to normal.

Affected

FinCEN systems (limited subset)

Actions

  • Monitor FinCEN's BSA E-Filing System website for updates on service availability.
  • Check for extended reporting deadlines if you are a qualifying filer.
  • Verify if your organisation relies on FinCEN services and plan for potential delays.
