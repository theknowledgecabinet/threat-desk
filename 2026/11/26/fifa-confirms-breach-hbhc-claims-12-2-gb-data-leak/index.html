FIFA confirms breach; HBHC claims 12.2 GB data leak

FIFA has confirmed unauthorised access to personal and business data, though it has not verified the Handsome Boy Hacking Club's claim of a 12.2 GB archive.

FIFA confirmed on 25 November that hackers gained unauthorised access to a limited set of personal and business information. The organisation stated it is working with relevant authorities, including law enforcement, and condemned the unlawful acquisition and publication of data. FIFA did not specify how the intrusion occurred, when it was detected, or which systems were affected.

The Handsome Boy Hacking Club (HBHC) claims to have obtained and published 12.2 GB of sensitive internal documents, including identity records, executive information, and financial files. Dark Web Informer reported that HBHC stated the files were taken from FIFA’s Microsoft environment and internal governance systems, though the claim remains unverified. The group also claimed it attempted to extort FIFA before posting the files on a cybercrime forum.

Cyber Security News noted that the claimed archive size is a poor measure of the breach’s impact, as large archives may contain duplicate or corrupted data. No public confirmation indicates that FIFA’s files were encrypted. If Microsoft cloud services were involved, audit logs could help investigators check for unusual sign-ins and trace which files were accessed or downloaded.

Affected

FIFA Microsoft environment, internal governance systems

Actions

  • Audit Microsoft cloud logs for unusual sign-ins and file access patterns
  • Verify the integrity and uniqueness of any claimed leaked data before assessing impact
  • Coordinate with law enforcement and relevant authorities for incident response
