NCSC issues guidance to counter agentic offensive frameworks

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre has published an eight-point plan to help defenders mitigate the surge in automated attacks from agentic offensive frameworks.

The NCSC published new guidance on 24 November 2026 to address the growing threat of autonomous offensive frameworks (AOFs) such as ARTEX, Cairn, PentAGI, RedAmon, Xalgorix, and Darkmoon. While these tools originated as legitimate penetration testing utilities, the agency noted they are now being used by threat actors to execute low-cost, automated attacks at scale.

According to the NCSC, these frameworks do not typically exploit novel or known vulnerabilities. Instead, they target 'low-hanging fruit' such as API weaknesses, default or leaked credentials, and misconfigurations in infrastructure as code. Cybersecurity intelligence firm Infrawatch has tracked 10,848 services across 8,105 hosts running these AOFs, highlighting the breadth of the exposure.

The agency's eight-point plan focuses on foundational hygiene rather than advanced AI defence, which it acknowledges will take time to mature. Key recommendations include rigorous asset management to identify externally accessible infrastructure, locking down perimeter services, and ensuring error messages do not leak information that agents can use to plan subsequent moves.

Further measures include deploying bot challenges, applying rate limits at the API level, and throttling account creation from disposable email services. The NCSC also advises placing honeytokens or tripwires somewhere no legitimate user would go to provide early warning and consume an agent's time and attempt budget.

Affected

Autonomous offensive frameworks (AOFs) such as ARTEX, Cairn, PentAGI, RedAmon, Xalgorix, and Darkmoon

Actions

  • Perform rigorous asset management and regular reviews of externally accessible infrastructure.
  • Deploy bot challenges and apply rate limits to API tokens and authenticated sessions.
  • Place honeytokens or tripwires somewhere no legitimate user would go to detect and delay agentic attacks.
  • Implement zero trust principles, including identity verification and least privilege access.
