Police disrupt 57 illegal streaming services in 11-country operation

Law enforcement from 11 countries disrupted 57 illegal IPTV services, identifying 321 websites and tracing over €26 million in cryptocurrency transactions.

Authorities from 11 countries, supported by Europol, the European Union Intellectual Property Office (EUIPO), INTERPOL, and the European Audiovisual Anti-Piracy Alliance (AAPA), have disrupted 57 illegal streaming services. According to a Europol press release, the operation identified 321 websites illegally offering copyrighted content, which collectively recorded over 7.3 million visits annually.

Investigators traced more than €26 million in cryptocurrency transactions, with further analysis ongoing. To achieve this, they partnered with crypto industry experts from Binance, Coinbase, and Chainalysis to link illegal IPTV services, crypto wallets, and the individuals controlling them. The operation targeted a market where providers sell unauthorised access to premium television channels, live sports, and films, often collecting payments via cryptocurrency to obscure the origin of profits.

The European Audiovisual Anti-Piracy Alliance (AAPA) stated that legitimate service providers lose around €4.83 billion annually as roughly 60 million people in Europe use illegal platforms. This action follows several recent operations, including Operation KRATOS 2 in June, which removed over 27,000 illegal streaming URLs and led to 29 arrests, and Operation Switch Off in January, which seized three industrial-scale illegal IPTV services.

Actions

  • Ensure corporate networks block access to known illegal streaming domains to reduce exposure to malware and phishing often associated with such sites.
  • Monitor cryptocurrency transactions for unusual patterns that may indicate illicit financial flows, leveraging tools from partners like Chainalysis.
  • Review content licensing agreements to ensure compliance with copyright laws and avoid legal risks associated with unauthorised content distribution.
