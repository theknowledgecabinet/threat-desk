Microsoft: Salt Typhoon and Crystal Typhoon using Artex AI tool

Microsoft reported that Chinese state-backed groups are using the Artex AI platform to automate network reconnaissance and exploitation at machine speed.

Microsoft said on Wednesday that Chinese state-backed groups Crystal Typhoon and Salt Typhoon have integrated Artex, an AI-powered platform, into their toolsets. The company stated the groups are using it for "network reconnaissance, credential attacks, and web application exploitation," including searching public repositories for exposed secrets and scanning internet-facing systems.

According to Microsoft, Artex allows threat actors to define specific goals and use AI agents to advance from reconnaissance to persistent, privileged access in a matter of minutes. The tool does not introduce new attack techniques but uses common penetration testing tools, often transmitting distinctive characteristics associated with those tools.

Microsoft noted that while Artex does not hide its activity by default, threat actors can vary network indicators like source IP addresses or user agent strings to bypass static detection. The most reliable signal of Artex activity is behavioural: it sends a wide variety of requests in a brief time and retries variations on the same technique at machine speed.

The FBI released an advisory two weeks ago warning that Artex is being used by cybercriminals and nation-states to breach government agencies and critical sectors. The tool was recently implicated in attacks on South Korean and Japanese financial institutions and a supply chain attack on ConnectWise.

Actions

  • Monitor for bursts of machine-speed activity and large volumes of anomalous traffic from a single source in a short time.
  • Analyse behavioural indicators rather than relying solely on static or simple signature-based detection.
