Bots spoof AI agent names to hunt for secrets

Cequence telemetry shows malicious bots have increasingly used AI agent names as cover to probe for secrets, while agents have moved from reading to signing in, booking and buying.

Security firm Cequence reported that malicious bots have adopted AI agent names as cover to probe for configuration files, keys and admin consoles. In its Agentic AI Security Index, published on 23 November, Cequence detailed telemetry collected between 16 May and 15 November 2026. Bot traffic using these identities climbed from fewer than 30 visits a day in May to a peak of approximately 1,450 in mid-October, with Cequence blocking 37,917 such visits over the period.

The report notes a shift in agent behaviour: among established AI agents, 13% of visits involved taking action in November, up from 1.4% in May. Action-taking visits, which include signing in, booking and buying, grew by 240%, while read-only visits fell by 73%. Cequence cautioned that agent names are self-reported and open to any bot, meaning the data does not implicate specific vendors like Perplexity, where 63% of claimed agent visits were probing for secrets.

Dana Simberkoff, chief risk, privacy and information security officer at AvePoint, stated that organisations need least-privilege access, identity controls and human approval for high-risk actions. Aviv Nahum, co-founder and CEO at Above Security, added that organisations must treat AI agents as "first-class identities and a new class of insiders." Cequence recommended governing agents by behaviour rather than by the names they claim.

Actions

  • Implement least-privilege access and strict identity controls for AI agents.
  • Govern AI agents by observed behaviour rather than self-reported names.
  • Require human approval for high-risk actions such as purchases or account management.
  • Monitor and audit agent traffic for anomalous probing of configuration files and admin consoles.
