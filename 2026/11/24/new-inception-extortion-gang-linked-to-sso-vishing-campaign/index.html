New Inception extortion gang linked to SSO vishing campaign

Okta and ReversingLabs link a new data extortion group to a voice phishing operation targeting single sign-on credentials, with Okta assessing the cluster very likely changed its brand affiliation from ShinyHunters.

A newly formed data extortion group named Inception has been linked to a large-scale campaign targeting single sign-on (SSO) credentials of global brands. The group surfaced on 17 November, claiming seven victims on its extortion site, including AMD, PepsiCo, Cigna Healthcare, AutoZone, and Lucid Motors, though none have publicly confirmed a breach. ReversingLabs says the gang has been operating since late October, identifying 21 domains used for SSO phishing and 41 domains believed to be part of the campaign infrastructure.

The campaign relies on phone-based social engineering rather than email phishing, with callers posing as support staff to pressure employees into resetting passwords via fake sign-in pages. These pages, often mimicking Okta or NetIQ/OpenText Access Manager, capture credentials before seeking additional multifactor authentication factors. ReversingLabs noted the lure sites are web-only and use legal-themed lookalike domains, bypassing email filters entirely.

Okta linked the operators to threat cluster O-UNC-053, previously associated with the ShinyHunters extortion group. Okta assessed that the cluster "very likely changed their brand affiliation" after law enforcement disrupted ShinyHunters in September. Inception disputes this link, claiming to operate independently, but Okta noted consistent infrastructure and tactics between the two entities. Neither Okta nor ReversingLabs could validate whether the data published by Inception is authentic.

Affected

Organisations using Okta or NetIQ/OpenText Access Manager for single sign-on

Actions

  • Enforce phishing-resistant authentication, including FIDO2 security keys and passkeys.
  • Strengthen help desk identity verification procedures before resetting passwords or MFA.
  • Review DNS, proxy, and identity provider logs dating back to 21 October 2026 for suspicious activity.
  • Limit sensitive applications to managed devices.
