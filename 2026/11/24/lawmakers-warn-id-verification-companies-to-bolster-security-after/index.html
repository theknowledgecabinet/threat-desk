Lawmakers warn ID verification companies to bolster security after IDScan breach

A bipartisan group of US federal lawmakers has written to identity verification companies, citing the IDScan breach that exposed over 150 million scanned IDs and pressing firms to provide detailed answers on data management.

Six US federal lawmakers, including Senators Brian Schatz and Marcia Blackburn, sent a letter to several identity verification firms on Monday, urging them to improve security practices following the September breach of IDScan. The incident resulted in the theft of customer data stored in the company’s cloud platform, leading to the exposure of scans for more than 150 million people’s drivers’ licenses and other identification documents, which subsequently appeared for sale on the dark web.

The letter highlights the risks of fraud and identity theft, noting that foreign adversaries can infer Americans’ behaviour through commercial data linked to government credentials. It specifically mentions that the exposed data included scans belonging to high-ranking military and intelligence officials, as well as nearly 600,000 medical cards and employment authorisations. The lawmakers stated that the scale of the breach warrants additional attention to the data-handling practices of these companies.

Independent journalist Brian Krebs, who first reported the IDScan breach, noted that cybercriminals on the Russia-tied dark web marketplace Nexus began exfiltrating the data more than a year before officials discovered it. Krebs also reported that many identity verification companies store scanned IDs by default, often requiring customers to pay extra to stop the collection. The lawmakers directed the firms to deploy zero-trust architecture, proactively hunt for vulnerabilities, and limit the collection, sharing, and storage of personally identifying information.

Affected

IDScan cloud platform; identity verification companies storing scanned IDs

Actions

  • Audit third-party identity verification vendors to determine if they store scanned IDs by default.
  • Negotiate contracts to prevent the continued collection and storage of identity scans after verification.
  • Implement zero-trust architecture to limit lateral movement in case of a breach.
  • Minimise the collection, sharing, and storage of personally identifying information.
