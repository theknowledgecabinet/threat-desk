AI-assisted campaign compromises Check Point management servers

GreyNoise reports a threat actor used AI-enabled tools to exploit and maintain persistent access to Check Point Security Management servers across multiple sectors.

Threat intelligence firm GreyNoise has identified a campaign, tracked as GreyNoise Threat 13 (GT13), targeting Check Point Security Management servers. The actor, active since early October, used 99 IP addresses, many associated with disposable infrastructure or compromised victim devices, to conduct scanning, exploitation, and control activities. GreyNoise believes the actor employed agentic harnesses to weaponise vulnerabilities and automatically exploit assets, complemented by hands-on-keyboard activity.

The campaign targeted at least 22 organisations in the energy, financial services, government, and manufacturing sectors across the US, UK, Australia, and other geographies. In one instance, the actor forged a signed admin session cookie to gain administrative access to a Rejetto HFS server, subsequently obtaining SSH access to scan the environment and exfiltrate data. The attacker used one victim’s server as a relay to store command results and data exfiltrated from 24 previously compromised Check Point servers.

Between 20 and 26 October, the actor issued a bulk certificate-management task to 24 servers. Although the task failed, the actor collected responses containing victim IP addresses in incrementing order, indicating an automated loop. GreyNoise confirmed activity between 17 October and 2 November but believes the campaign is ongoing due to persistent access. The actor attempted to exploit multiple vulnerabilities, including CVE-2026-61500 (Rejetto HFS), CVE-2023-4966 (Citrix NetScaler), and CVE-2026-16232 (Check Point SmartConsole).

Affected

Check Point Security Management servers; Rejetto HFS; Citrix NetScaler; Ivanti; Check Point SmartConsole; Linux; Windows; Microsoft Exchange; VMware vCenter

Actions

  • Audit Check Point Security Management servers for unauthorised SSH keys and administrative session cookies.
  • Review logs for bulk certificate-management tasks and unusual SCP transfers to external relay servers.
  • Patch or mitigate known vulnerabilities in adjacent infrastructure such as Rejetto HFS, Citrix NetScaler, and VMware vCenter.
  • Monitor for lateral movement and privilege escalation attempts originating from compromised management nodes.
