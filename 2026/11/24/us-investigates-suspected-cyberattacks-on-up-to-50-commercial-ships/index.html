US investigates suspected cyberattacks on up to 50 commercial ships

US authorities are investigating a wave of cyber intrusions and intimidation campaigns targeting vessels carrying hazardous cargo, with Iran and Russia under scrutiny.

According to a Wall Street Journal report, US officials believe as many as 50 ships carrying oil, liquefied petroleum gas, and chemicals may have been targeted. The investigation focuses on suspected state-sponsored activity, with Iran emerging as the primary suspect, although a Trump administration official told the WSJ that investigators have found no evidence linking Iran to the incidents and described four of the attacks as minor disruptions.

The incidents reportedly involved both cyber intrusions and intimidation campaigns, including bomb threats and ransom demands. The US Coast Guard has deployed specialised cyber protection teams to at least four vessels approaching US ports, including the VL Prosperity and Kohaku, to investigate suspected compromises of their onboard networks. One vessel, the Vivit Africa, experienced system problems and received a bomb threat while transporting LNG from the US to Italy, though it is not established that a cyberattack caused the technical incident.

Attackers are reportedly exploiting vulnerabilities in Fortinet networking devices widely used in the maritime industry. In September, the US Coast Guard Cyber Command and the FBI issued a joint alert warning shipping companies about state-sponsored actors reportedly exploiting FortiGate vulnerabilities in maritime environments. Maritime cybersecurity firm Cydome noted that most vessels have “less cyber protection than a midsize office,” highlighting the need to address exposed network devices and improve separation between IT and OT environments.

Affected

Fortinet FortiGate devices

Actions

  • Review and patch Fortinet FortiGate devices for known vulnerabilities.
  • Strengthen remote access controls for onboard systems.
  • Improve separation between IT and OT environments.
