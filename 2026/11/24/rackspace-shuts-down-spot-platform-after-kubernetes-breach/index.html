Rackspace shuts down Spot platform after Kubernetes breach

Rackspace is permanently shutting down its Spot cloud platform after a breach allowed attackers to access customer Kubernetes clusters and secrets, forcing users to migrate by 8 December.

Rackspace has announced it will discontinue the Rackspace Spot cloud platform following a security incident that compromised customer environments. The company stated that it made the decision to wind down the service to provide a clear path forward for customers after reviewing the situation. The platform, which allows organisations to deploy and manage containerised applications using Kubernetes, will be permanently discontinued on 8 December 2026 at 5 pm Central Time, at which point all remaining workloads will be terminated and customer data will no longer be accessible.

According to Rackspace, hackers breached the Spot platform on 17 November using compromised credentials. This allowed them to create unauthorised pods in customer Kubernetes clusters, read Kubernetes Secrets, and list nodes and volumes. The intrusion was discovered on 18 November following a customer report, leading Rackspace to disable access to the platform’s administration console. Service was restored on 21 November after an extended outage, though the company noted that an unauthorised party may have read cluster Secrets and files on storage volumes.

Rackspace has stated that the affected Spot environment is segmented from its core infrastructure and that it has not identified evidence of unauthorised access to its core systems or the environments of other Rackspace customers. However, the company is instructing all Spot customers to treat their API tokens, refresh tokens, kubeconfigs, and Kubernetes Secrets as potentially compromised, regardless of whether they previously received a breach notification. Customers have been advised to review access logs dating back to 16 November and search for suspicious Kubernetes objects associated with the attack, including specific IP addresses and pod prefixes.

Affected

Rackspace Spot cloud platform

Actions

  • Treat API tokens, refresh tokens, kubeconfigs, and Kubernetes Secrets as potentially compromised and rotate them.
  • Review access logs dating back to 16 November for suspicious activity.
  • Search for suspicious Kubernetes objects with prefixes i1, glm-, and probe.
  • Migrate workloads and download data before the 8 December 2026 shutdown deadline.
