US lawmakers propose CISA board to review major cyber incidents

A new bill seeks to establish a National Cyber and AI Investigations Board within CISA to analyse root causes of significant cyber incidents without assigning liability.

Representatives Morgan Luttrell (R-TX) and Ritchie Torres (D-NY) introduced the ROOT CAUSE Act on 18 November, proposing the establishment of a National Cyber and AI Investigations Board (NCAI Board) within the US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA).

According to Infosecurity Magazine, the board would examine significant cyber incidents affecting US organisations, identify root causes, and recommend preventive measures. The legislation specifies that the NCAI Board would have no regulatory or enforcement authority, and its reviews would not assign civil or criminal liability.

The bill, introduced through House Homeland Security Republicans, was one of four new proposals on 18 November. The others include the AI INSIGHT Act for securing AI agents on federal networks, the BRIDGE AI Act for recruiting private-sector AI specialists to CISA, and the SECURE IT Act, which would designate the Department of Homeland Security as the Sector Risk Management Agency for the Information Technology Sector.

Actions

  • Monitor legislative developments regarding the proposed NCAI Board.
  • Review internal incident response plans to ensure they align with potential future review criteria.
  • Assess third-party risk management processes in light of the proposed SECURE IT Act.
