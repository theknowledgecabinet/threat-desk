RansomwareHelp CTO indicted for wire fraud after FBI sting

The co-founder of a firm claiming to decrypt ransomware without payment has been charged with wire fraud after an FBI operation revealed he was secretly paying attackers and charging victims a markup.

Juan Ricardo Palacio Escobar, the chief technology officer and co-founder of Florida-based RansomwareHelp, has been indicted for wire fraud in Rhode Island. The company, which operated for six years, advertised 'advanced cryptographic tools' that could restore 100% of data without paying ransoms, claiming a 99% success rate and a team of 80 experts. However, the indictment alleges that Palacio routinely paid ransomware groups for decryption keys and charged victims significantly more than the amount paid to the threat actors.

The FBI conducted a sting operation in which agents posed as both a ransomware victim and a fictitious ransomware group. When the fake victim contacted RansomwareHelp, the company reiterated its policy against paying ransoms. Shortly after, Palacio contacted the FBI’s fake ransomware group, negotiating a decryption key for approximately $6,000 in bitcoin. He then billed the fake victim $16,010, including a diagnostic fee and the decryption cost.

Paul Ihme, co-founder of security firm Soteria, described a 2024 incident where RansomwareHelp proposed a $438,880 recovery fee for a Lockbit infection, citing '510 preconfigured forensic servers' and 'brute force processes'. Ihme noted that RansomwareHelp insisted on remote access via Anydesk, which he believes was used to delete ransom notes and prevent the victim from contacting the attackers directly. The victim eventually engaged a different firm and paid half of RansomwareHelp’s proposed fee.

Actions

  • Verify the credentials and methodology of any third-party recovery firm before granting remote access.
  • Do not delete ransom notes or attacker contact details without forensic analysis, as they may be required for negotiation or attribution.
  • Be wary of firms claiming '100% data recovery' without ransom payment, as this often indicates they are acting as intermediaries for the attackers.
