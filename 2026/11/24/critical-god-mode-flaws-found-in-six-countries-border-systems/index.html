Critical 'God Mode' flaws found in six countries' border systems

Independent researcher Eaton Zveare discovered critical API vulnerabilities in the online visa and immigration systems of Nigeria, Ethiopia, Fiji, Zambia, Sri Lanka, and Ghana, allowing unauthorised access to sensitive data and administrative controls.

Security researcher Eaton Zveare identified critical API vulnerabilities, including broken object-level authorisation (BOLA), broken function-level authorisation (BFLA), and missing authentication, in the online passport and border-control systems of six nations. The flaws, which he described as 'God mode at the border,' allowed unauthorised access to millions of travellers' sensitive visa and passport data, potentially including biometric information such as fingerprints. In some cases, the vulnerabilities granted access to administrative functions that could be used to approve or deny entry into a country.

Zveare stated that the systems were built by third-party contractors with varying levels of expertise, which likely contributed to the security gaps. In Nigeria's case, he noted that an over-reliance on AI to build the systems may have introduced flaws into the code. He discovered the initial vulnerability in Nigeria's private back-end portal by searching Microsoft Bing for 'admin panel' approximately a year ago, before expanding his investigation to five other countries using similar web technologies such as Angular and React.

The researcher developed exploits to demonstrate that attackers could view personally identifiable information (PII) and access administrative functions. Zveare warned that a worst-case scenario could involve an attacker identifying a high-level executive, accessing their travel itinerary and hotel information, and using those details to track their movements for potential kidnapping or assassination. He will disclose further details of the flaws during a session at Black Hat Europe on 10 December.

Affected

Online visa and immigration systems of Nigeria, Ethiopia, Fiji, Zambia, Sri Lanka, and Ghana

Actions

  • Audit third-party contractors and their development processes for critical infrastructure projects.
  • Implement strict authentication and authorisation controls to prevent BOLA and BFLA vulnerabilities.
  • Ensure national CERTs have clear protocols for responding to disclosures regarding critical border systems.
