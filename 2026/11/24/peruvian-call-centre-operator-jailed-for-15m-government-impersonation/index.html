Peruvian call centre operator jailed for $15m government impersonation fraud

A Lima-based operator was sentenced to eight years for managing call centres that defrauded over 30,000 Spanish-speaking US residents.

Carla Magaly Alcedo Mendoza, a 45-year-old call centre operator from Lima, Peru, pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit mail and wire fraud. According to court documents, she managed and operated Peruvian call centres linked to large-scale fraud and extortion schemes between January 2013 and December 2018.

The scheme targeted Spanish-speaking US residents, initially offering free services before impersonating US government officials and lawyers to demand payment. The Department of Justice stated that the operation defrauded more than 30,000 victims out of $15 million, using threats of court action, arrest, and immigration consequences. Alcedo was extradited to the United States in April 2025 following her arrest in March 2023.

This conviction is part of a broader enforcement effort; Alcedo is the tenth defendant extradited from Peru in this case. The US Federal Trade Commission reported in June 2026 that Americans lost $3.5 billion to impersonation scams in 2025, with approximately $920 million stolen by government impersonators.

Actions

  • Verify the identity of callers claiming to be government officials or lawyers before discussing financial matters.
  • Do not pay demands based on threats of arrest or immigration action received via unsolicited phone calls.
  • Report suspected government impersonation scams to the relevant national consumer protection agency.
