Serial-to-IP gateways broadcast lack of authentication to internet

Research by Flare reveals that a significant proportion of exposed OT serial gateways publicly report that authentication is disabled, creating a direct path for attackers to access industrial machinery.

Adrian Cheek, senior cybercrime researcher at Flare, conducted an internet scan using a specific vendor's UDP discovery service and identified 2,474 serial-to-IP gateways exposed on the web. According to the report, these devices appeared to be real hardware rather than honeypots, with half located in Russia. When probed, approximately half of the devices responded by disclosing their authentication status. Of those that responded, 973 reported that authentication was disabled, meaning 39.3% of all exposed gateways visibly lacked authentication, and 77% of those whose status could be determined were unprotected.

Cheek noted that these devices often ship with authentication off and are configured by integrators who then leave them unattended for 10 to 15 years. Operators may avoid enabling security features because older SCADA packages and virtual serial port drivers may not support secured modes, risking the loss of the link to the field. Furthermore, firmware updates at remote sites carry the risk of breaking functionality if no spare hardware is available, leading operators to avoid touching working devices.

The study highlighted specific risks to mobile network operators and private access point name (APN) service providers, noting that 322 of the discovered devices resided in address space associated with known mobile network operators in Europe and the Middle East. Cheek referenced the previous year's Polish power grid attack, where a private APN was used to pivot from a wind farm to a power plant, ultimately allowing attackers to shut down a steam turbine, water treatment system, and seven serial-to-IP devices due to missing network isolation.

Affected

Serial-to-IP gateways (specific vendor's UDP discovery service)

Actions

  • Inventory all cellular units and verify in writing with carriers whether devices on private networks can reach each other or other customers' devices.
  • Configure IP restrictions so only the SCADA host can reach serial ports and only an engineering workstation can reach the management interface.
  • Disable unused services such as the discovery service, Telnet, older SNMP, and the web interface.
  • Remove SSH and Telnet entirely from any device facing the carrier network and place a strict firewall between the gateway and the control network.
