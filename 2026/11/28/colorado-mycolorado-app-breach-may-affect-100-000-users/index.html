Colorado myColorado app breach may affect 100,000 users

The Colorado Governor’s Office of Information Technology confirmed a data breach in the myColorado application, with approximately 100,000 individuals potentially affected.

The Colorado Governor’s Office of Information Technology (OIT) disclosed that unauthorised access occurred in the myColorado mobile application, which serves approximately 2.1 million users for digital identification and state services. OIT identified the incident after attackers triggered automated threat detection systems within its Amazon Web Services environment, leading to the application being taken offline last week.

The investigation determined that attackers accessed the ‘myCards and Documents’ section, compromising user-uploaded data such as PDF files, photos of government IDs, vehicle registrations, and insurance cards. OIT stated that approximately 100,000 individuals might have been affected, though no payment information was accessed. Staff are currently matching the accessed documents to individual users to refine these estimates.

The myColorado application remains offline with no restoration date announced. OIT executive director Sarah Tuneberg said the state is prioritising direct notification of affected users and restoring the app with security enhancements in place. Direct notifications to affected individuals are expected to begin on 27 November.

Affected

myColorado application

Actions

  • Monitor for direct notification from the Colorado OIT regarding potential exposure of personal documents.
  • Review AWS CloudTrail and security logs for unauthorised access patterns in similar state or enterprise cloud environments.
  • Verify that automated threat detection systems are active and alerting on anomalous access to sensitive data stores.
