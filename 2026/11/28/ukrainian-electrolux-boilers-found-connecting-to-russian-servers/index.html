Ukrainian Electrolux boilers found connecting to Russian servers

Mysterium VPN researchers identified Electrolux-branded smart boilers in Ukraine communicating with a Moscow-based server, raising privacy concerns regarding household data and remote control.

A Ukrainian user, Alexander Unger, observed his Electrolux EWH 80 water heater attempting to connect to mqtt.cloud.rusklimat.ru. Mysterium VPN’s research team verified the finding, identifying the server as belonging to Rusklimat, a Moscow-based climate equipment group, hosted on Yandex Cloud infrastructure in Russia.

The investigation revealed that the device uses the Hommyn app, which is linked to Rusklimat. The manual for the EWH SI EEC series names CladSwiss AG of Zug, Switzerland, as the manufacturer and states that the boiler is made in China. It notes that the Electrolux name is used under license from AB Electrolux, the Swedish parent company, and that the remote-control app, ClimatOn, comes from CladSwiss. However, the Hommyn app’s privacy policy states that personal data is stored exclusively within Russia under Russian law, and the app sends device history and location data to Rusklimat servers.

Mysterium VPN noted that the same communication channel used for data transmission also carries commands to switch the boiler on or adjust its temperature. The researchers emphasised that they did not connect to the servers or examine the physical device, so they cannot confirm the full scope of data exfiltration, but the connection to a Russian server in a jurisdiction not recognised by the European Commission as providing adequate data protection is a significant concern.

Affected

Electrolux EWH 80, Electrolux SmartInverter series, Ballu, Zanussi, Royal Thermo devices using Hommyn or ClimatOn apps

Actions

  • Check router logs for connections to 'rusklimat' or use DNS monitoring tools like Pi-hole or AdGuard Home.
  • Block the Russian server at the router level to stop remote access.
  • Disable the boiler's Wi-Fi or place smart appliances on a separate guest network.
  • Note that a VPN on the user's device does not affect where the appliance sends data; the fix must be at the router.
