DWP takes online services offline after cyber incident

The UK Department for Work and Pensions has taken its online services offline following a 'cyber incident', with security experts suggesting the department detected active hacking activity.

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) shut down access to its online benefits and pensions services late on Thursday, citing a 'cyber incident'. The department has not specified the trigger for the shutdown but has not ruled out that it was targeted by hackers. Work and Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden described the event as a 'serious cyber incident', while the DWP characterised the closure of online services as a 'precaution'.

Security experts suggest the department may have detected intruders within its systems. John Curry, a cyber security expert at Bath Spa University, told BBC News the DWP had 'almost certainly' detected hacking activity and shut down systems to prevent data theft. He noted that if hackers are discovered inside a network, the 'best thing to do is slam the front door shut'. David Knott, the UK's former chief technology officer, expressed his 'strong suspicion' that the department was targeted, noting that taking down citizen-facing services implies an attack could be mounted through the 'front door'.

The government stated it is working with GCHQ's National Cyber Security Centre to investigate the incident. Downing Street confirmed that other government departments had not been accessed by a 'malicious actor'. The DWP said it does not believe any personal information has been lost or stolen, and assured claimants that payments would continue as normal, with no sanctions for those affected by the service disruption.

Affected

DWP online benefits and pensions services

Actions

  • Monitor official DWP channels for service restoration updates
  • Verify that internal systems are not part of the affected perimeter
  • Review incident response plans for potential lateral movement from government infrastructure
  • Communicate with stakeholders that payment delays are not linked to the cyber incident
