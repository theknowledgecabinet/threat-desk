ICO reprimands Home Office over Helios border data errors

The UK data protection regulator has reprimanded the Home Office for failing to ensure the accuracy of personal data in its new border control system, which could potentially have resulted in wrongful detentions.

The Information Commissioner’s Office (ICO) issued a reprimand to the Home Office for infringements of Articles 5(1)(d) and 25(1) of the UK GDPR and sections 38(1) and 56(1) of the Data Protection Act 2018. The regulator found that the department failed to ensure the personal information processed by its new Helios border control platform remained accurate and up to date.

According to the ICO, the Home Office introduced the system on 2 October 2024 without a dedicated mechanism for reporting and correcting inaccurate personal information. While the previous system had a dedicated team for real-time corrections, the new regime required inaccuracies to be reported to the owner of the source data. The ICO noted that staff were instructed not to include personal information when filing reports, making it "virtually impossible" to provide the details needed to correct a record.

As a result, many inaccuracies were left unchecked. The ICO stated that this failure could potentially have resulted in serious consequences, including people being wrongly detained for additional checks or refused entry to the UK. The regulator noted this was likely to cause psychological distress to affected individuals. On 25 October 2025, the Home Office introduced a temporary reporting system; by March 2026, border force officers had raised 6,500 incidents through this system, with around 5,000 incorrect records removed.

Affected

Helios border control information platform

Actions

  • Ensure data protection by design includes clear processes for reporting and correcting inaccurate personal data.
  • Verify that staff have effective mechanisms to report data inaccuracies without being prohibited from including necessary identifiers.
  • Review federated systems to ensure robust, effective ways to identify and correct inaccurate information without delay.
