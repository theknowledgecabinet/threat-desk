Bluetooth flaws in Elecbrakes controllers allow remote brake manipulation

Researchers demonstrated that unauthenticated attackers within 100 metres can engage or release trailer brakes, posing a severe physical safety risk.

Australian consultancy Element Digital identified three vulnerabilities in Bluetooth-enabled brake controllers manufactured by Elecbrakes. The flaws include two critical issues involving missing authentication and insufficient replay-attack protection, which allow an unauthenticated attacker within Bluetooth range (up to 100 metres) to manipulate braking force or engage/release brakes without victim interaction.

CISA published an advisory last week noting the affected units are primarily used in Australia and New Zealand. The researchers warned that forcing a trailer’s brakes to maximum while the tow vehicle continues moving could cause a jackknife, a highly dangerous accident scenario. A third medium-severity flaw in the firmware update mechanism could enable persistent compromise, though researchers noted this risk remains theoretical as they did not demonstrate full impact.

Elecbrakes released firmware patches for the EB2 and VM1 products in early July after being notified in mid-May. The vendor stated that 60–70% of daily active controllers have installed the update, which requires physical pairing for remote access. No evidence of malicious exploitation has been found, but the vendor is still developing a remediation plan for the older ELBC 2000 model.

Affected

EB2, VM1 and ELBC 2000 brake controllers

Actions

  • Install the latest firmware updates for EB2 and VM1 controllers.
  • For ELBC 2000 owners, maintain an active connection between the controller and smartphone or wireless remote during use.
  • Monitor for vendor announcements regarding the ELBC 2000 remediation plan.
