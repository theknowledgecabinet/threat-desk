Carnegie Endowment donor data leaked via Soapbox Engage breach

A cyberattack on third-party fundraising platform Soapbox Engage exposed donor contact details and giving history for the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace.

The Carnegie Endowment for International Peace confirmed that a cyberattack on its third-party vendor, Soapbox Engage, resulted in the leakage of donor information. The incident occurred on 28 October, and the organisation stated that threat actors were removed from the vendor’s system on 30 October. Spokesperson Sharmeen Aly confirmed that the breach affected a small percentage of the database, exposing names, email addresses, phone numbers, and giving history, but no full credit card numbers.

Carnegie notified all individuals in its database regarding the breach, citing European data privacy laws, and hired a cybersecurity firm to verify the removal of the attackers. The incident coincides with recent security disclosures from other digital donation platforms, including DonorPerfect and GoFundMe. GoFundMe stated it identified two separate attacks in October and November, while The Nonprofit Times reported that these attacks on fundraising platforms appeared to be coordinated, with at least one incident involving the theft of funds.

Soapbox Engage did not respond to requests for comment. GoFundMe said it had disabled unauthorized accounts and is monitoring for further suspicious activity, noting that the recent attacks highlight the need for better protection of donor information across the sector.

Affected

Soapbox Engage

Actions

  • Audit third-party integrations and vendor access to sensitive donor or customer data.
  • Monitor for unauthorized access or anomalous activity in fundraising and payment systems.
  • Review data minimisation practices to limit the exposure of sensitive personal information.
