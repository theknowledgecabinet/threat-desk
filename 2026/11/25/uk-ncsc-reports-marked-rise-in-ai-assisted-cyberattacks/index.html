UK NCSC reports marked rise in AI-assisted cyberattacks

Britain's National Cyber Security Centre warns that AI agents are accelerating attacks on existing weaknesses, urging organisations to reinforce basic security controls.

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) stated on Tuesday that it has observed a "marked rise" in cyberattacks assisted by artificial intelligence since September. The agency explained that while AI is not rendering established cybersecurity practices obsolete, it is reducing the margin for error for organisations that have failed to implement basic defences. The NCSC noted that these tools increase attackers' speed, scale, and persistence while lowering costs, largely due to the availability of hacking frameworks combined with cheap open models or illegal API resellers.

The blog post, co-authored by NCSC chief technology officer for architecture David Chismon, identified Artex as the predominant framework currently receiving attention. It also named Cairn, PentAGI, RedAmon, Xalgorix, and Darkmoon as other tools in use. The NCSC emphasised that the weaknesses being exploited are not new or novel, and that the tools provide no new capability beyond what typical threat actors already possess. However, compared with human attackers, these agentic frameworks work more quickly, are exhaustive in their searches, and learn instantly from failures.

NCSC chief executive Richard Horne warned that the threat is increasing because previously tolerated weaknesses can now be discovered and exploited at scale. Chismon noted that AI is likely to benefit attackers more than defenders in the short term, as autonomous defensive systems are not yet mature enough to be relied upon. The agency added that while current AI-driven activity is noisy and easy to detect, future frameworks may prioritise stealth, making detection more difficult.

Actions

  • Implement cybersecurity best practice to prevent exploitation of known weaknesses.
  • Review and secure forgotten keys and unprotected APIs, which can now be found in minutes.
  • Monitor for noisy activity associated with agentic offensive frameworks.
  • Assess organisational resilience against persistent, automated attackers.
