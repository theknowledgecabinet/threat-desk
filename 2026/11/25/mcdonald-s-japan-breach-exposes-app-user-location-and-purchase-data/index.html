McDonald's Japan breach exposes app user location and purchase data

McDonald's Japan confirmed a database breach affecting its official mobile app, exposing customer location data and purchase histories without revealing the scale of the incident.

McDonald's Japan disclosed on Friday that threat actors gained unauthorised access to a database associated with the company's official smartphone app. The company stated that the compromised data includes users' product purchase history, IP addresses, mobile device location data, and customer management IDs. It confirmed that names, email addresses, phone numbers, home addresses, and credit card information were not exposed.

The company has not disclosed when the intrusion was detected, how many individuals were affected, or who was responsible. McDonald's Japan said a follow-up investigation found no service disruption or misuse of the official apps as a result of the incident. It is currently working to invalidate credentials used for the unauthorised access and has reported the incident to Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission.

This incident follows a wave of cyberattacks targeting Japanese organisations, including a recent breach at Burger King Japan. A researcher at Macnica noted that attackers are increasingly probing websites and APIs for access-control and authentication weaknesses, and exploiting known vulnerabilities to find other victims.

Affected

McDonald's Japan official smartphone app database

Actions

  • Monitor for unauthorised access attempts in application databases.
  • Review and invalidate authentication credentials associated with compromised systems.
  • Report incidents to relevant data protection authorities as required by local law.
