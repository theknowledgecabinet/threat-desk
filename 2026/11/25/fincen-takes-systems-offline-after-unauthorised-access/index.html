FinCEN takes systems offline after unauthorised access

The US Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network has taken some systems offline as a precaution following a cybersecurity incident involving unauthorised access to a limited subset of its infrastructure.

The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) stated on 25 November 2026 that it is investigating a cybersecurity incident that resulted in "unauthorised access to a limited subset of its systems." As a proactive measure, the agency has taken some services offline, though it confirmed that the Bank Secrecy Act Electronic Filing (BSA E-Filing) System remains operational.

FinCEN, which collects suspicious activity reports and other Bank Secrecy Act filings from banks and other financial institutions, has extended deadlines for required document submissions. The agency advised that filers needing to expedite the delivery of suspicious activity information to law enforcement can use the Financial Institutions Hotline, which is not impacted by the service disruption.

This incident occurs while several other US federal agencies, including the FBI, the CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, are reportedly investigating data breaches targeting criminal records and unclassified intelligence information.

Actions

  • Monitor FinCEN communications for updates on BSA E-Filing system status.
  • Use the Financial Institutions Hotline (1-866-556-3974) to expedite suspicious activity information if required.
  • Review internal filing schedules to account for extended deadlines for required documents.
