Turnitin detects unauthorized activity in customer-facing systems

Educational technology firm Turnitin has disclosed a cyberattack affecting its customer-facing systems, with no confirmation yet of data theft or service disruption.

Turnitin disclosed that it detected unauthorized activity involving certain customer-facing systems and subsequently deployed its incident response plan. According to a public statement from CEO Chris Caren, the company is investigating the incident with the assistance of external cybersecurity specialists. Law enforcement and cyber regulators have been notified of the breach.

The company stated that its systems remain fully operational and safe to use. Turnitin noted that the affected systems do not collect government identification credentials or institutional financial information. The firm has not disclosed the nature or scope of the unauthorized activity, nor whether any user data was accessed or stolen.

As of 24 November 2026, no ransomware groups have publicly claimed responsibility for the attack. Turnitin continues to monitor its systems for further unauthorized activity, acknowledging that it is too early to guarantee the threat has been eliminated. The company’s status page shows no incidents for November 2026, and uptime history logs no disruptions.

Affected

Turnitin customer-facing systems

Actions

  • Monitor Turnitin status pages for service disruptions.
  • Review internal logs for unusual access patterns to Turnitin integrations.
  • Wait for official vendor communication regarding data exposure before issuing user alerts.
