US investigates potential Iranian link in FortiGate breaches of 50 commercial ships

US authorities are investigating whether Iranian state actors exploited known Fortinet FortiGate flaws to access the operational technology networks of up to 50 commercial vessels.

A US official told NPR that hackers exploited known flaws in Fortinet FortiGate devices used aboard vessels, with US intelligence estimating up to 50 commercial ships may have been compromised. The Wall Street Journal reported that the targeted ships carried oil, liquefied petroleum gas, chemicals, and other hazardous cargo, including at least one liquefied natural gas tanker. Authorities have not publicly attributed the breaches to Iran, and the figure remains an intelligence estimate rather than a confirmed count.

The FBI and US Coast Guard warned in September that attackers were exploiting FortiGate flaws in the maritime sector, specifically targeting unpatched devices. According to the US official, hackers moved from onboard information technology networks into operational technology systems that monitor and control physical equipment. While at least one ship experienced an extended communications blackout, the agencies stated there were no current reports of operational disruption, vessel instability, physical danger to crews, or environmental harm.

The investigation highlights the risk of connected business and control networks, where access to IT systems can provide a pathway to OT systems. Although reaching a ship’s control network does not prove that anyone took control of the vessel, it demonstrates why email or login problems can warrant a wider investigation. A 2021 CISA advisory documented Iranian government-backed hackers exploiting Fortinet flaws against critical infrastructure, though this does not establish the attribution for the current maritime incidents.

Affected

Fortinet FortiGate devices

Actions

  • Patch FortiGate devices to close known entry points.
  • Review firewall and VPN logs to determine if access occurred before patching.
  • Ensure business networks are separated from systems that run ship equipment.
  • Reset credentials that may have been exposed and require multifactor authentication for remote access.
