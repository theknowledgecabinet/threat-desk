Bulgaria reveals five-year government data breach

Bulgarian authorities discovered a cyberespionage campaign active since 2021 that exfiltrated 130 GB of sensitive state documents, highlighting critical gaps in national security monitoring.

Bulgaria’s Ministry for Innovation and Digital Transformation announced the discovery of a hacking operation that had been running undetected since 2021. According to Reuters, more than 130 GB of documents, equating to approximately 62 million pages, were downloaded from government databases over the five-year period. The stolen files reportedly include cabinet documents, reports, and communications between ministries and government agencies.

The intrusion was only identified in August after the introduction of a new national cybersecurity monitoring system, which flagged suspicious activity. Minister Ivan Vassilev stated that there is currently no evidence that the personal data of Bulgarian citizens was compromised. While several international hacking groups are suspected, investigators have not yet linked the attack to a specific country or nation-state actor.

In response, authorities isolated affected systems, blocked attacker access, and removed malware. Intelligence services, the defence ministry, police, and international partners, including the EU’s cybersecurity agency, joined the response. Prosecutors have been informed to investigate both the external attackers and potential internal failures in detecting the intrusion earlier.

Affected

Bulgarian government administrative networks

Actions

  • Implement continuous network monitoring to detect long-term intrusions
  • Audit administrative networks for unauthorised access and data exfiltration
  • Isolate compromised systems immediately upon detection of suspicious activity
