Chinese hackers use Cairn AI to find zero-day in medical imaging app

Amazon Threat Intelligence says a Chinese-nexus actor allegedly used the autonomous AI tool Cairn to discover and exploit an unpatched path traversal flaw in MONAI Label, breaching East Asian universities.

Amazon Threat Intelligence reported that a suspected Chinese-linked threat actor used the open-source autonomous AI penetration-testing framework Cairn to attack universities in East Asia between 8 and 10 September 2026. The attackers left a publicly accessible instance of Cairn without authentication, allowing Amazon to view operational history, including prompts written in Simplified Chinese and timestamps set to China Standard Time.

The AI agents were instructed to "obtain shell access" from specific IP addresses. While attempts to exploit Log4Shell and brute-force credentials failed, the agents allegedly discovered a path traversal vulnerability in MONAI Label, an open-source medical imaging application. Amazon assessed with high confidence that this was a zero-day, as no prior advisory or fix existed. The agents exploited the flaw to write a Python reverse shell to the sitecustomize.py file, gaining root access within 11 minutes of mapping the API.

The attacker subsequently used the same vulnerability to breach a second university network. Amazon contacted the maintainers of MONAI Label, who released a security update on 18 November 2026. Amazon also worked with a government partner to notify the affected organisations, though it declined to name the specific universities or their country.

Affected

MONAI Label (open-source medical imaging application)

Actions

  • Apply the security update released by MONAI Label maintainers on 18 November 2026.
  • Monitor for automated AI-driven attacks that may outpace manual triage capabilities.
  • Ensure AI penetration-testing tools like Cairn are not left publicly accessible without authentication.
