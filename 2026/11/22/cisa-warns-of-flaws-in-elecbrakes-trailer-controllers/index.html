CISA warns of flaws in Elecbrakes trailer controllers

Three vulnerabilities in Elecbrakes EB2, VM1, and ELBC2000 devices could allow attackers to manipulate trailer brakes or firmware via Bluetooth.

CVE-2026-124281CVE-2026-125273CVE-2026-96805

The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) has issued an advisory for three vulnerabilities affecting Elecbrakes electronic trailer brake controllers. The flaws, identified as CVE-2026-124281, CVE-2026-125273, and CVE-2026-96805, expose the devices to unauthorised firmware modification, command replay attacks, and unauthenticated command injection.

According to the advisory, successful exploitation could allow an attacker to gain control of the trailer brakes, modify firmware, disable brake lights, or alter brake conditions. The most severe issue, CVE-2026-96805, permits an in-range Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) device to write to the control interface without authentication or pairing, carrying a CVSS 4.0 base score of 8.5 (high). CVE-2026-125273, which lacks replay or command-integrity protection, also scores 8.5 (high), while the unprotected firmware channel (CVE-2026-124281) is rated 5.3 (medium).

CISA lists these vulnerabilities as not currently known to be exploited. The affected equipment is deployed in transportation systems across Australia and New Zealand. While fixes are available for the EB2 and VM1 models, no remediation is currently available for the ELBC2000, which is no longer sold.

Affected

Elecbrakes EB2 <=3.0.4.2.0; Elecbrakes VM1 <1.3.4.2.1; Elecbrakes ELBC2000 vers:all/*

Actions

  • Update Elecbrakes EB2 firmware to version 3.0.4.4.3 via the Elecbrakes app.
  • Update Elecbrakes VM1 firmware to version 1.3.4.2.1 via the Elecbrakes app.
  • Monitor vendor communications for a fix for the ELBC2000, as no patch is currently available.
  • Restrict physical and wireless access to the controllers to mitigate unauthenticated BLE attacks.
