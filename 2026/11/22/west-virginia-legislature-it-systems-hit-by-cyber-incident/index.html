West Virginia Legislature IT systems hit by cyber incident

The West Virginia state legislature has disabled access to certain IT systems following a cyber incident that began on Thursday, with no group yet claiming responsibility.

Ann Ali, deputy chief of staff for the House of Delegates, told Recorded Future News that the attack started on Thursday and continued through Friday. Upon discovery, relevant teams contained the incident and began an investigation with outside cybersecurity specialists.

In connection with the investigation, access to certain Legislature IT systems has been temporarily disabled. Ali stated that, based on current knowledge, the incident appears to be limited to the Legislature’s computer systems, with no other state agencies or offices impacted. She did not respond to follow-up questions regarding whether the incident involved ransomware.

No hacking groups have taken credit for the attack as of Friday. The West Virginia legislature is split between a Senate and a House of Delegates, comprising 134 total members representing the state’s 1.7 million residents.
