WhatsApp patches IP leak flaw uncovered while disrupting scam campaign

WhatsApp has fixed a low-severity vulnerability in its Android apps that it believes a scammer may have exploited to make scam messages seem more credible.

CVE-2026-23871

WhatsApp’s security team identified CVE-2026-23871 while disrupting a scam campaign. The flaw, an incomplete validation of external ad-reply URLs, allowed an attacker to reveal a target’s IP address by sending media from an attacker-controlled URL within a crafted Click to WhatsApp ad-reply message. A WhatsApp spokesperson stated the company believes a scammer was using it to make their scam message seem more credible.

The vulnerability affects WhatsApp and WhatsApp Business for Android versions prior to 2.26.17.4. It carries a CVSS 3.1 base score of 3.5 (low) and is not listed by CISA as known exploited. WhatsApp noted that the issue may have been abused in conjunction with specific spam messages.

In the same advisory, WhatsApp disclosed two medium-impact vulnerabilities: CVE-2026-102769, an SQL injection bug in Android versions prior to 2.26.35.74 that could lead to file deletion, and CVE-2026-44908, a flaw in the remote-attestation verifier for Meta AI’s confidential computing backend. WhatsApp stated it has seen no evidence that either of these was exploited.

Affected

WhatsApp for Android and WhatsApp Business for Android before 2.26.17.4

Actions

  • Update WhatsApp and WhatsApp Business for Android to version 2.26.17.4 or later.
  • Monitor for unexpected outbound connections or IP address exposure in Android environments.
  • Review ad-reply message handling in any custom integrations with WhatsApp Business APIs.
