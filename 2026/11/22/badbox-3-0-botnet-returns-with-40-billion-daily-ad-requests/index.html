BADBOX 3.0 botnet returns with 40 billion daily ad requests

HUMAN researchers report that the BADBOX malware botnet has returned, generating a record 40 billion fraudulent bid requests per day from infected Android devices.

According to HUMAN's Satori team, BADBOX 3.0 has become the largest documented ad-fraud operation by daily bid request volume. At its peak, the botnet accounted for roughly 40 billion bid requests a day, representing more than 4% of all CTV traffic observed by the Human Defense Platform. This volume is more than three times that of the previous record holder, VASTFLUX.

The malware infects cheap Android phones, tablets, and streaming TV boxes running the Android Open Source Project (AOSP). It performs ad fraud by playing video ads in hidden windows and impersonating legitimate streaming apps. HUMAN also observed fraudulent server-side ad insertion requests that tricked advertisers into believing ads were played on devices that did not exist. Additionally, a proxying module enrolled infected devices in residential proxy networks, including PXYEDGE, a rebranded successor to IpMoYu.

HUMAN linked the malware to a specific Chinese electronics manufacturer through domain analysis and code checks. The organisation participated in a recent disruption operation that took down multiple command-and-control domains and one malware repository. HUMAN warns that operators are likely to adapt to these disruptions, as they have done in the past.

Affected

Android Open Source Project (AOSP) devices, including cheap phones, tablets, and streaming TV boxes

Actions

  • Purchase certified Android devices only from reputable manufacturers.
  • Ensure Google Play Protect is available and active on all devices.
  • Monitor for unexpected residential proxy traffic or high-volume ad requests in network logs.
