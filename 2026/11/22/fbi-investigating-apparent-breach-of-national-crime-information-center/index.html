FBI investigating apparent breach of National Crime Information Center

The FBI is investigating a second reported intrusion in two months, this time targeting the NCIC database used for sharing criminal justice records across US law enforcement.

According to CNN, the FBI is investigating an apparent breach of its National Crime Information Center (NCIC) system, citing two sources briefed on the matter. The NCIC is an FBI-operated database used by local, state, and federal law enforcement agencies to share information on wanted persons, outstanding warrants, stolen vehicles, and firearms. Police officers routinely access the system during traffic stops, criminal investigations, and background checks.

The intrusion was reportedly discovered in recent days, though it remains unclear when the breach occurred, how attackers gained access, or whether any data was stolen. The FBI has not publicly confirmed the incident and did not respond to requests for comment from CNN. It is currently unknown whether the attackers accessed sensitive records or if the intrusion affected the system's availability to law enforcement agencies.

This incident follows a claim by the ShinyHunters extortion group two months prior that they breached FBI systems by exploiting an unpatched Oracle PeopleSoft vulnerability on a third-party recruitment platform. While the FBI subsequently arrested suspected ShinyHunters members, including a Canadian cybersecurity executive, there is currently no indication that the latest NCIC incident is connected to that earlier breach.
