Dragos finds prompt theft at three AI inference providers

Threat actors stole customer prompts and credentials from at least three AI inference providers, with none detecting the intrusions until notified by researchers.

Cybersecurity firm Dragos reported that threat actors breached at least three AI inference providers and stole customer prompts, but none of the victims detected the intrusions until they were notified. The discovery followed a test where a team led by Dragos chief research officer HD Moore sent roughly two million prompts containing unique bait, such as URLs, hostnames, and AWS keys, to widely used AI services between July and November.

Moore stated that the bait prompts were processed alongside real customer requests, which were "almost certainly copied too". The largest breach involved roughly 200 AWS test keys stolen in late October. Dragos observed the attackers checking which stolen keys worked and attempting to use them to obtain valuable data from associated accounts. The firm noted that some stolen keys came from requests rejected before a model ran, indicating the attacker was copying requests at the provider’s front door rather than deeper in the inference stack.

OpenRouter, an AI gateway, said its own systems were not breached but removed three unnamed providers in response. Phala admitted that requests sent between 21 October and 8 November should be considered exposed after an attacker compromised a server running a DeepSeek model. Together AI revealed that an AI coding agent leaked credentials on GitHub, which were used to obtain stored prompt samples, while Novita and Atlas Cloud traced potential exposure to requests reaching the API of model developer MiniMax.

Affected

AI inference providers (unspecified), OpenRouter (indirectly), Phala, Together AI, Novita, Atlas Cloud, MiniMax

Actions

  • Audit API logs for unauthorised access to prompt data and credentials.
  • Monitor for the use of previously exposed AWS keys or credentials in the wild.
  • Review third-party AI gateway configurations and provider security postures.
  • Rotate credentials and secrets that may have been processed by compromised inference services.
