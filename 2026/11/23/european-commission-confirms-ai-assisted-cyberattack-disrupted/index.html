European Commission confirms AI-assisted cyberattack disrupted services

The EU executive body confirmed an AI-assisted attack in late October disrupted key systems, though no staff data breach was found.

The European Commission has confirmed it was the target of an AI-assisted cyberattack detected on 22 October and confirmed on 26 October. Spokesperson Thomas Regnier stated that the attack has been largely contained, with no evidence of a data breach affecting Commission staff. As a protective measure, several applications were temporarily taken down, most of which have since been restored.

According to Politico, the incident caused disruptions lasting over 10 days and potentially compromised large amounts of data. Impacted services included the Commission’s HR platform Sysper, the medical reimbursement system MyPMO, and translation systems. Regnier did not attribute the incident to a known group and said he was not aware of any ransom demand.

The disclosure follows a warning from CERT-EU regarding threat actors using the AI-powered penetration-testing framework Artex. CERT-EU assessed that Artex’s public availability would likely accelerate adoption by threat actors, resulting in more frequent, faster, and more scalable automated attacks. Artex has previously been used in attacks against Barracuda, Railway, and ConnectWise customers.

Affected

European Commission HR platform Sysper, medical reimbursement system MyPMO, translation systems

Actions

  • Monitor for indicators of compromise related to AI-assisted penetration testing frameworks such as Artex.
  • Review access logs for HR, financial, and translation systems to detect unauthorised activity.
  • Ensure temporary service disruptions are documented and recovery procedures are tested.
