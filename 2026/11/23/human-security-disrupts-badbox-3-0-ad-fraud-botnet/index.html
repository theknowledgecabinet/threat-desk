Human Security disrupts BadBox 3.0 ad fraud botnet

The third iteration of the BadBox operation generated 40 billion daily bid requests by spoofing CTV apps on low-cost Android devices.

Bot and fraud protection firm Human Security says it has disrupted BadBox 3.0, the largest ad fraud botnet by bid volume. At its height, the operation accounted for roughly 40 billion bid requests a day and more than 4% of all CTV traffic observed by the Human Defense Platform, a volume three times that of the VastFlux scheme.

The botnet targets low-cost, uncertified Android Open Source Project devices infected with backdoors. These devices run fraud modules that render hidden video ads in a background window, mimicking legitimate video ad SDKs to make bid requests appear to come from known applications. The threat actors also use server-side ad insertion to monetise this application spoofing.

Human Security’s Satori Threat Intelligence and Research Team linked the operation to Chinese electronics manufacturer TXCZ, noting that modules check for TXCZ build properties and a TXCZ domain serves payload downloads. The firm also identified an actively maintained catalogue of fraud modules on public repositories, including residential proxy modules that enrolled devices into eleven networks, including PXYEDGE.

Affected

Low-cost, uncertified Android Open Source Project devices

Actions

  • Monitor for hidden video ad rendering or unusual CTV traffic patterns.
  • Block known command-and-control domains associated with BadBox 3.0.
  • Audit Android devices for uncertified hardware or TXCZ build properties.
  • Filter bid requests for anomalies in residential IP addresses and app spoofing.
