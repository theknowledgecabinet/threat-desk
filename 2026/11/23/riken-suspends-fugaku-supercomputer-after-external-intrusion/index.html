Riken suspends Fugaku supercomputer after external intrusion

Japan’s Riken institute has cut off external access to the Fugaku supercomputer after detecting suspicious traffic that allowed an outside party to tamper with internal servers.

Riken suspended operations of the Fugaku supercomputer and severed external access after detecting suspicious traffic on its user authentication system. In a notice dated 17 November, the institute stated that an outside party used a public dashboard displaying Fugaku’s operational status to breach the system’s internal operations network.

The intrusion resulted in the tampering of multiple servers. Riken said that the names, email addresses, and user IDs of up to 4,597 Fugaku and RIKYU users may have been exposed.

Affected

Fugaku supercomputer, RIKYU users

Actions

  • Audit public-facing dashboards and status pages for potential entry points into internal networks.
  • Review authentication logs for suspicious traffic patterns on user authentication systems.
  • Verify the integrity of internal servers following any detected external intrusion.
