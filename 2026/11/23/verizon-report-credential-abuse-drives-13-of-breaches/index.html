Verizon report: Credential abuse drives 13% of breaches

Verizon's 2026 Data Breach Investigations Report identifies credential abuse as the initial access vector in 13% of breaches.

According to BleepingComputer, Verizon’s 2026 Data Breach Investigations Report found that credential abuse was the initial access vector in 13% of breaches. The report also noted that vulnerability exploitation accounted for 31% of breaches. These findings underscore the continued reliance on compromised or weak credentials for unauthorised access to corporate systems.

The article highlights that manual tracking methods, such as spreadsheets, often fail to capture the full scope of an organisation's credentials. It notes that employees frequently store passwords in browsers, chat messages, or personal vaults due to convenience, creating blind spots for administrators. Verizon’s 2025 report, cited in the text, indicated that in the median case, only 49% of users’ passwords were unique across services, with 123456 identified as the most common corporate password by NordPass.

Ransomware groups, such as Pysa, are reported to use automated scripts to scan drives for files containing terms like “password” or “login.” If attackers locate such documents, they may encrypt them or use the contained credentials to access other accounts, potentially spreading the attack further. The source advises that organisations require structured approaches to manage credential visibility, strength, and ownership.

Actions

  • Implement a centralised password manager to track credential strength, ownership, and access.
  • Identify and remediate weak, reused, or exposed passwords using automated scanning tools.
  • Establish clear ownership and access rules for shared accounts and non-password secrets like API keys.
