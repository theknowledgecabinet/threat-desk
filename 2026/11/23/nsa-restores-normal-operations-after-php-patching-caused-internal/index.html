NSA restores normal operations after PHP patching caused internal network outages

The US National Security Agency has returned to normal operations after patches to PHP caused disruptions to its internal classified communications network, NSANet, for three days.

The National Security Agency (NSA) confirmed it is now operating under normal conditions following three days of disruptions to NSANet, the internal network used for classified communications. The outages were attributed to maintenance affecting PHP, a commonly used programming language. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton stated that patching following the maintenance caused some systems to fail to restart as expected.

While the agency has resumed business as usual, certain facilities remain under “Code Orange” for non-essential employees, requiring delayed openings and supervisor check-ins. A Friday morning voicemail indicated the agency was operating under “normal conditions, with exceptions.”

Administration officials insisted the incidents were not the result of a cyberattack and were not connected to recent IT issues affecting Microsoft products, airline travel, or online banking. Sources familiar with the situation reported that intelligence operations at NSA Georgia, focused on the Middle East, were not negatively impacted, and the outages did not curtail support for President Donald Trump’s trip to Asia.

Affected

NSANet, PHP

Actions

  • Verify that patching procedures for widely used languages like PHP include rollback plans and testing in non-production environments.
  • Monitor internal communications networks for unexpected outages following routine maintenance or patch cycles.
  • Confirm that operational security and intelligence functions remain unaffected during IT maintenance windows.
