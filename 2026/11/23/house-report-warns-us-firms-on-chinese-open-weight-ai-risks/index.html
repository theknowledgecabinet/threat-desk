House report warns US firms on Chinese open-weight AI risks

A joint congressional report urges US companies to stop embedding Chinese open-weight AI models, citing national security risks and the difficulty of removing such dependencies.

A 50-page joint investigative report from the House Homeland Security Committee and the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party warns that US companies are increasingly embedding China-developed open-weight AI models into their tech stacks. The report identifies Airbnb, DoorDash, and Anysphere (owner of the Cursor coding platform) as examples of firms using these models for customer assistance, coding, and inventory management. None of the firms immediately responded to a request for comment.

The report draws on hearings, classified briefings, and agency advisories, stating that the investigation uncovered risks that "American businesses, policymakers, and national security officials can no longer afford to overlook or downplay." It compares the situation to the rapid adoption of Huawei and ZTE technology, warning that removing Chinese open-weight models from US infrastructure could take several years and cost taxpayers billions.

According to the report, federal investigators found that Chinese developers used proxy networks and fraudulent accounts to harvest outputs from American frontier models to replicate their capabilities. The report includes 12 policy recommendations, such as building an American open-weight AI initiative, creating an AI bill of materials, and barring China-developed models from federal contract work and sensitive critical infrastructure functions.

Actions

  • Review tech stacks for embedded Chinese open-weight AI models.
  • Assess the difficulty and cost of removing such dependencies.
  • Monitor for policy changes regarding federal contracts and critical infrastructure.
